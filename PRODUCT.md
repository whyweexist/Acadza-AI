# PRODUCT — Acadza Report → Fix

**Who loses the most?** Students repeatedly encountering a broken problem, especially those whose confidence and scores are affected by a wrong answer key. One incorrect item can confuse thousands of learners while one vague report disappears in a manual queue. The console groups reports by canonical question `_id`, prioritizes verified grading risk and repeated impact, and gives faculty the original MathML, the proposed *smallest* patch, independent evidence and student-specific reply in one screen. Counting reports helps urgency but is never treated as proof.

**What never auto-applies?** AI cannot invent diagrams, distractors, a new question, a syllabus decision or chapter remapping. All taxonomy `auto:false` repairs require faculty work. A weakly substantiated stem value, disputed physics solution, invalid scoring type, stale question hash, ambiguous string replacement or disagreeing solvers also blocks automation, *even when the taxonomy technically allows that repair type*. An approved patch remains a proposal until a separate version-checked publishing system confirms a real write. Students must never be told a change is already live when it is not.

**Week-one metric:** **Faculty-confirmed harmful defect time-to-resolution**, measured from the earliest associated report to verified live publication, with a p90 split by wrong-key versus other defects. This measures the actual student harm window; approval counts alone do not.

**Beyond the brief:** Add a **question exposure impact tracker**: when faculty verifies a harmful answer-key defect, identify attempts made during the affected version, transparently correct impacted scoring and notify only affected students. This closes the learning and trust loop rather than merely fixing future attempts.

**Product trade-off:** Prefer a small queue of traceable, safe actions over a large number of unverified AI auto-edits. The 44-result sample is precomputed for demonstration, not an audited outcome or proof of measured accuracy.
